# Suspension and GVM options for the Kia Tasman X-Pro (research, 2026-10-31)

The fit-out puts a lot of permanent weight over and behind the rear axle. This page covers:
- what that weight does to the factory suspension;
- which Tasman-specific kits exist so far;
- what fits this build.

**[est]** marks an estimate. Prices are from retailer pages on the dates checked, and many kits were backordered.

## 1. The load this build puts on the vehicle

Kia AU MY26 figures for the X-Pro (from [`research-notes.md`](research-notes.md)): GVM 3250 kg, kerb 2237 kg, payload 1013 kg, rear axle (GAWR) 2002 kg, front 1450 kg, GCM 6200 kg.

| Scenario (v0.14 model) | Total | vs GVM 3250 |
|---|---|---|
| Kerb + fit-out (550 kg) + gear and water (154 kg) + 2 adults (160 kg) | ~3101 kg | 149 kg spare |
| The same with 4 adults | ~3261 kg | **11 kg over** |
| 2 adults + bull bar (~75) + tow bar (~30) **[est]** | ~3206 kg | 44 kg spare |
| 4 adults + bull bar + tow bar | ~3366 kg | **~116 kg over** |

**Rear axle:** the model puts **506 kg of the 550 kg fit-out on the rear axle**, plus about 170 kg of gear. That's roughly **650–700 kg on the rear springs, permanently**, before people or a tow bar.
- The rear axle rating (2002 kg) probably still has margin. The kerb split per axle isn't published, so weigh it.
- The springs are the problem, not the rating: see section 2.
- Snow trips add more: chains, recovery gear, extra water, firewood. All of it sits at the back.

## 2. Why the factory rear won't like it

Kia's local suspension engineer explained the Tasman rear to AutoExpert. It's **sprung in stages, on purpose**:
- a soft four-leaf main pack for unladen ride;
- extra leaves that engage as load builds, through polymer dampers;
- the **bump stop as the third spring stage** under heavy load.

Unladen there are only **about three fingers** of clearance to the bump stop. With about 260 kg on the towball it sat on the stops almost constantly, and Kia says that's intended ([AutoExpert](https://autoexpert.com.au/blog/kia-tasman-towing-on-bump-stops-not-a-defect)). Reviewers have also called the rear "too soft for loads" ([4x4 Australia](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/reviews/2025-kia-tasman-review)).

About 650 kg of permanent rear load would keep the factory rear in its stiff second or third stage all the time:
- worse ride, and more noise and vibration in the cabin;
- less travel left for bumps;
- the tail sits down, so the headlights aim high.

So **this build needs rear springs rated for a constant load**, whether or not the GVM goes up.

## 4. What fits this build

1. **Weigh it first.** Do it now, unladen with a full tank, front and rear axle separately, at a public weighbridge (about A$30). Then repeat after stage 1–3 with the bay, slides, lid and tent fitted. The model's 506 kg rear figure is good, but the kerb split per axle isn't published.
2. **Most likely: a GVM upgrade, with Pedders GVM+ as the only Tasman option found so far.**
   - The build already lands within about 150 kg of 3250 kg with two people. It goes over with four people, a bull bar, or snow-trip gear.
   - Take the **600 kg rear constant-load** springs, which match the ~650 kg permanent rear load.
   - Ask Pedders:
     - which GVM rating applies to the **X-Pro**;
     - what happens to the **GCM** (probably unchanged at 6200, so every extra kg of GVM comes off what you can tow);
     - the ride height with the 600 kg springs on the X-Pro.
   - It's federally approved, so it can go on now or later. Fitting it before any other suspension work avoids paying twice.
3. **If you stay under 3250 kg:** buy a load-rated kit instead, picking the **heaviest rear spring** (about 500 kg constant).
   - **Dobinsons IMS** has the 500 kg rear.
   - **Tough Dog** has a heavy rear on request.
   - **OME MT64** is worth watching once ARB publishes its Tasman springs.
   - Treat a 40–50 mm lift as a side effect, not the goal.
4. **Keep any lift small.**
   - The X-Pro already sits about 14 mm higher on its suspension than other grades, and has 252 mm of clearance.
   - About 170 kg of this build sits high up (tent, lid cargo, roof, visor). More lift raises the centre of gravity, worsens body roll and CV angles, and needs the ADAS camera and radar recalibrated.
   - In most states, more than 50 mm of suspension lift, or 75 mm combined with tyres, needs engineering certification.
5. **Airbags only for levelling**, on top of a correct rear spring. Use them for a trailer on the ball, or a full jerry and firewood. They don't raise the legal limit, and the springs should carry the permanent load.
6. **Shocks:** choose a large-bore touring damper (foam cell or monotube). The rear will carry heavy loads on corrugations. MT64, IMS, Tough Dog foam cell and Pedders foam cell all qualify.
7. **Paperwork:**
   - Fit a new GVM plate or label with the kit.
   - Tell your insurer.
   - Keep the receipts. Under Kia's 7-year warranty, a claim on a related part can be argued if Kia shows the modification caused the fault.

## 5. Questions to ask before buying

- **Pedders:**
  - Which GVM rating applies to the X-Pro?
  - Does GCM stay at 6200 kg, and what's the new braked tow limit?
  - What's the laden and unladen ride height with the 600 kg rear?
  - Is a pre-registration version available, or post-registration only?
- **TJM:** Is an XGS kit, or a Lovells GVM kit, coming for the Tasman, and when?
- **ARB:** What are the OME Tasman spring options, constant-load kg and lift?
- **All of them:** Is ADAS recalibration needed? Is the headlight aim reset included?
