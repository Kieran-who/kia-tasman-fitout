## 2. Slides: how they carry the weight

- **4 runners per slide.**
  - Outer pair: Accuride DZ9308, which lock in and out.
  - Inner pair: DZ9301, back to back on a 6 mm centre keel.
  - All four are 1118 mm (44"). Rating about 2 × 218 kg moderate use.
- **Stowed:** the carriage drops onto UHMW stop blocks, so road shock bypasses the runners. The front of the cassette bolts to the battery bay frame. The bay is pinned to the front tie-downs, so the batteries act as counterweight.
- **Extended:** two fold-down legs at the rear of each slide take heavy static loads such as a running microwave or a full fridge being packed.
- **Modules:** the 15 mm ply deck has a 100 mm grid of countersunk stainless receivers and a flush L-track down the centre.
  - Suggested male part: an 8 mm ball-lock pin (push-button release) to locate each module and take shear.
  - Hold-down: an L-track stud or Camloc quarter-turn fastener.
  - Details in v0.2.

## 3. Electrical system

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  ALT[Alternator / start battery] -- 50 mm² pair, 150 A fuse --> ORION[2 × Orion XS 50 A]
  ROOFPV[Roof 2 × 190 W] --> MPPT1[MPPT 100/50]
  TENTPV[Tent 2 × 100 W] -- detachable --> MPPT2[MPPT 75/15]
  SHORE[240 V shore inlet - under tent] -- detachable --> MP
  ORION --> LYNX
  MPPT1 --> LYNX
  MPPT2 --> LYNX
  BATT[2 × iTech400X Pro 800 Ah] -- MRBF each --> ISO[Isolator] --> CT[Lynx Class-T 400 A] --> LYNX[Lynx Distributor]
  BATT --- SHUNT[SmartShunt]
  LYNX -- 2 × 50 mm² --> MP[MultiPlus-II 12/3000]
  LYNX --> DCFB[DC fuse block]
  DCFB --> AND[Anderson × 3, 12 V, USB-C: rear panel + slide + cabin]
  DCFB --> FRIDGE[Fridge 12 V]
  DCFB --> STAR[Starlink Mini 12-48 V]
  DCFB --> TENT12[Tent 12 V - detachable]
  MP -- AC out --> RCBO[RCBO per circuit]
  RCBO --> GPO1[Rear panel GPO × 2]
  RCBO --> GPO2[Slide GPO]
  RCBO --> GPO3[Tent GPO - heater - detachable]
  CERBO[Cerbo GX] -. VE.Bus / VE.Direct .- MP
  CERBO -. Wi-Fi via Starlink .- HA[Home Assistant]
```

- **Inverter: MultiPlus-II 12/3000/120-32.**
  - 3000 VA, **2400 W continuous at 25 °C** (2200 W at 40 °C), 5500 W peak.
  - Its 120 A charger and auto transfer switch make the 240 V inlet under the tent work. PowerAssist lets a weak campsite supply be topped up from the batteries.
  - **It runs a kettle or a microwave, not both at once.** True 3000 W+ continuous needs the Quattro 12/5000 (4 kW, 34 kg, 444 × 328 × 240). That would make the bay about 340 deep and the slides about 40 mm shorter. See question 4.
- **Home Assistant:** Cerbo GX → local MQTT → Home Assistant's core *Victron GX* integration (read + control). Victron BLE is the fallback when there is no network.
- **Tent umbilical:** connectors live on the **underside of the load lid near its front (hinge) edge**, inside the tub, so they stay dry and secure. The tent's cables pass through the lid in a **split cable-entry gland** (e.g. icotek KEL), so plugs don't have to fit through a hole.
  - Circuits: 240 V out (heater), 240 V shore in, 12 V Anderson, PV Anderson.
  - To remove the tent: open the lid, unplug four connectors, pop the gland frame.
  - This avoids running anything outside between the cab and tub. Tell me if you'd rather do that.
- **Leaving the tub:** the factory outlet is removed and its harness path reused for:
  - the alternator feed to the engine bay
  - the cabin feed to under the rear seat (default: Anderson + USB-C PD + 12 V socket)
  - the roof PV feed
- **240 V compliance:** every inverter output circuit gets its own labelled RCD/RCBO (AS/NZS 3001.2). Fixed 240 V wiring must be installed or certified by a licensed electrician. The design keeps all 240 V in IP66 Clipsal 56-series hardware so it's straightforward to sign off.

## 4. Roof

- **Platform: Wedgetail Adventure 1600 × 1450** (Tasman kit WTM-KT-1614).
  - **2 × Victron 190 W** panels (1485 × 668) recessed inside the frame on Wedgetail solar brackets.
  - Flush light bar in the front plate.
- **Cargo:** two **removable cargo bars on risers** clamp into the side-rail channel about 130 mm above the panels. Snowboards and kayaks ride on the bars, never on the glass. The bars come off when not needed, keeping the roof quiet and the solar fully exposed.
- **Starlink Mini:** fixed at the rear edge of the platform on a low bracket, cable down the back of the cab.
- **Tent top:** 2 × Sunman eArc 100 W bonded to a 3 mm ACM sheet across the two top bars, about 10 kg total of the 20 kg allowance. It feeds its own MPPT so the arrays don't drag each other down when one is shaded or the tent is open.

| Slides 2 × (4 runners + frame + ply + track) | 80 |
| Power cabinet + outlets | 10 |
| Water tank + 80 L water | 92 |
| Load-Lid (est) | 60 |
| Tent | 84 |
| Tent solar + backing | 10 |
| Roof platform + panels + light bar + Starlink + cargo bars (est) | 61 |
| **Fit-out total** | **≈ 535** |
| Fridge (loaded) + microwave (est) | 70 |
| **Total before people and other accessories** | **≈ 605** |

- **Payload is about 1013 kg**, leaving about **400 kg** for occupants, gear and any bull bar, tow bar or steps. With a bull bar (the photo shows one) and four adults, **you'll be at or over GVM**. Options:
  - travel with less water
  - a GVM upgrade, if one becomes available for the Tasman
  - drop the tent-top or roof solar weight
- **Roof:** a 100 kg dynamic limit minus about 61 kg of platform and gear leaves about **39 kg of cargo**. Snowboards are fine. Two typical kayaks (20–25 kg each) are not, unless they're light inflatable or composite boats, or carried elsewhere.
- **Load-Lid:** 200 kg dynamic vs about 105 kg for the tent, solar and bars. OK.

## 6. Questions for feedback (v0.1 → v0.2)

1. **Overall layout:** front battery bay, two full-length slides, passenger rear power cabinet plus charging board over the arch, and an L-shaped water tank on the driver side. Happy?
2. **Clear height of about 450 mm** above the slide decks (deck top 98 mm). A lower deck (about 75 mm) is possible by dropping the centre keel, but the slides lose about half their capacity. Keep it strong, or go lower?
3. **Slides:** both at 100% extension (1118 mm), with fold-down legs. Do you want the driver slide identical, or shorter or lighter?
4. **Inverter:** MultiPlus-II 12/3000 (2400 W continuous, one big load at a time) or Quattro 12/5000 (4000 W, +15 kg, about A$2,100 more, slides about 40 mm shorter)?
5. **Tent:** which end is the front? I've assumed the ladder and fold-out are at the **rear**. Where exactly do you want the 240 V outlet and inlet (on the fixed base, under the fold-out)? Is the lid-underside connector panel with a split gland OK?
6. **Water:** about 80 L usable (80 kg) or smaller? Pump and tap at the rear driver corner, plus a hose reel. OK?
7. **Module receivers:** ball-lock pins (push-button) or a quarter-turn (Camloc) latch? Any module sizes you already know you want (e.g. a 2-drawer unit, a kitchen box)?
8. **Cabin outlets under the rear seat:** what do you want there?
9. **Accessories already fitted** (bull bar, tow bar, steps, winch)? I need them for the payload check.
10. **Roof cargo:** kayak type and weight?
