# v0.12 concept: cable chain under the power deck, full-width slides

Status: **twelfth iteration.** The interference check reports **0 clashes** (travel and camp). Tub, arch and fridge dimensions are still `[EST]` until the measurement sheet comes back.

Renders: [`renders/v0.12/`](../renders/v0.12) (section through the chain and fridge, power slide out, plan)

## 1. Cable chain moved under the deck (owner request)

| | v0.11 | v0.12 |
|---|---|---|
| Chain | igus E2.15, R75, standing upright in a 50 mm lane outboard of the power slide | Same chain **lying on its side** under the power deck, on a 3 mm glide tray. Both runs sit side by side, bending in plan |
| Power deck width | 529 mm | **579 mm** (+50 mm, same as the driver slide) |
| Fridge-well floor | 12 mm above the tub floor | **54 mm** (the chain passes under the pan: tray 6–9, chain 9–47, 4 mm gap, 3 mm pan) |
| Fridge-well width | 423 mm | 473 mm |
| CFX3 45 top vs the lid | 60 mm clear (on floor L-tracks) | **18 mm clear** (stands on the pan; the straps over it leave 15 mm) |
| Tallest fridge that fits | ~530 mm | **~490 mm** |
| Fridge tie-down | 2 L-tracks on the pan floor | 4 flush folding tie-down rings riveted to the pan (tracks would cost 10 mm of height) |

**Why the chain lies on its side.** Standing upright, its loop is 2R + 20 = 170 mm tall, and only about 80 mm is free under the deck. A tighter bend radius would damage the 6 mm² and 230 V high-flex cables, which need about 75 mm. On its side the chain is 38 mm tall, and the loop takes about 190 mm of width instead of height.

**How it's fixed:**
- **Moving end:** a 4 mm bracket hangs from the carriage's front cross member and reaches 115 mm back, under the well pan. Its loop clears the battery bay by 30 mm when stowed.
- **Fixed end:** at the centre of travel, bolted through the tray to a base cross tie.
- **Length:** travel/2 + πR ≈ 0.82 m, as in v0.8.

**Inspection:** slide the power slide fully out. The tray, the fixed run, the loop and both brackets are then open from above inside the tub, so there's no deck hatch.

**Checked in the model:**
- The stowed fold-out legs stay behind the chain's moving end at every point of travel.
- The carriage cross members (underside 62 mm) and the pan (51 mm) clear the chain (top 47 mm).
- The UHMW stops and the base plates are outside the tray band.

## 2. Pod feed moved to the battery bay

With the lane gone, the power slide's side plates sit against the passenger cabinet wall, so cables can't enter from the side any more. They now enter at the **front** of the slide, from the bay.

- **DO2 (pod 12 V):** now on an **MRBF 30 A terminal fuse (F17)** on the system + busbar's spare stud. It drops down the 40 mm gap behind the inverter, enters the front of the cassette at floor level, and runs along the tray outboard of the fixed run to the chain. It is 3.25 m long, voltage drop about 0.3 V at 15 A. **FB1 way 5 is now spare.**
- **AC3 (pod 230 V):** goes back from RCBO 2 to the bay alongside AC1, then follows the same path (6.0 m).
- The cabinet's low side gland to the slide is gone.

## 3. Outlet and feed changes (since v0.11)

- **Power-slide pod:** Anderson, 12 V and 230 V only. The dual USB-C was removed.
- **Rear panel USB-C:** 2 × Treklo dual 120 W, each with one **100 W PD** port and one **20 W** port. Feed DO1C upsized to **4 mm² / ATC 25 A**: both units flat out draw about 22 A, which overloaded the old 10 A fuse.
- **Cabin:** no outlets. DO3 (15 A) feeds the under-seat **compute box** for the smart-ute system through an **SB50 inlet**, then a Smart BatteryProtect and per-device fuses. The tablet runs from the Tasman's own USB-C.
- **Smart switching:** reduced to rockers for **Starlink (S4)** and **tent 12 V (S5)**, plus a Home Assistant-only cut for the camera switch. Details in [`smart-switching.md`](smart-switching.md).
