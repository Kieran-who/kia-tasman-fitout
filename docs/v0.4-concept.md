## 1. Under-mount slides: can the runners go under the deck?

**Yes, but only with rails that are rated lying flat.** Ordinary heavy drawer runners lose most of their capacity when laid flat:

| Rail | Section | Flat rating at ~1.2 m | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuride 9301 / 9308 (v0.3) | 76 × 19 | **−80 %** flat (≈ 36 kg/pair). Accuride limits flat mounting to ≤ 711 mm; Häfele says the 9308 isn't for flat use | ✗ |
| Chambrelan RA series | – | No flat rating; Chambrelan warns of significant deflection flat | ✗ |
| Fulterer / igus drylin | – | No flat rating / ~25 kg | ✗ |
| Rollon DE35 | 34 × 35 | 1045 N per rail, ~34 mm sag at 150 kg | ✗ (too soft) |
| **Rollon DE43 (L1170)** | **44 × 43** | **C0rad 2022 N per rail lying flat**, measured fully extended. Pair ≈ 412 kg static. At 150 kg on the tip: safety factor ≈ 2.75 (Rollon recommends 2–3.5 with vibration) and **~15 mm tip sag** | ✓ **chosen** |
| Rollon DE63 | 58 × 63 | 6026 N per rail, ~3 mm sag | ✓ but 20 mm taller |
| Commercial 4WD fridge/cargo slides | 74–146 mm tall | 90–350 kg | ✗ (taller than the side-mount design) |

**v0.4 design (modelled):**
- **2 × Rollon DE43 under each deck**, at 20 % and 80 % of the deck width.
  - The fixed member sits on 3 mm packers on the floor ribs, bolted through the floor with spreader plates.
  - The moving member is screwed to the underside of the deck.
- **Deck top = 3 + 43 + 1 + 15 mm ply = 62 mm** (was 84 mm), leaving **486 mm** clear under the lid.
  - **The fridge well is gone.** A 476 mm 45 L fridge stands straight on the deck, so the receiver grid covers the whole power slide.
- **The two decks sit 6 mm apart.** Modules on the two slides can butt up against each other.
  - Driver deck 579 mm wide, power deck 534 mm.
  - The power deck gives up a 45 mm lane on its outboard edge for the upright cable chain.
- **Rear roller supports:** three sealed 608-bearing rollers on the cassette at the tub edge carry the deck as it slides out. That cuts the load and sag on the rails.
  - **Pin-in legs** still take heavy loads at full extension (they also kill the ~15 mm sag).
- **Lock:** DE rails have no lock, so each deck gets a **spring-plunger lock** at the rear handle that drops into a floor plate (stowed) or a stop (extended).
- **Weight:** the slides drop from ~59 kg to **~48 kg** for both.

**The catch is cost.** Rollon DE43 lists at roughly US$1,000 for a 450 mm rail, so an L1170 rail is likely **US$1.5k+ each, and you need 4.** Get Australian distributor quotes before committing.
- **Budget fallback:** the v0.3 side-mount DZ9308 design (deck at 84 mm, fridge well, ~A$350/pair, built-in lock in/out). The model can switch back if the quotes are painful.

## 2. Solar and alternator charging: what we have and why

| Source | Charger | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Alternator | **Victron Orion XS 12/12-50A** (input limited to 25–30 A for now) | Has no solar input |
| Roof 190 W + 130 W | **Victron SmartSolar MPPT 100/30** (was 100/50; 320 W ≈ 23 A, same case, cheaper) | Own MPPT |
| Tent 2 × 100 W | **Victron SmartSolar MPPT 75/15** | Own MPPT; detaches with the tent |

So **no, it's not a combined unit**, on purpose:
- **Combined units cap the total.** The Redarc BCDC Alpha 50 (A$899) takes up to 1000 W of solar and uses "Green Power Priority" (solar first, alternator tops up), but **total output is capped at 50 A**. Projecta IDC50X, Enerdrive DC2DC 40A+ and Renogy DCC50S are similar. With separate units, outputs **add up**: ~25–50 A from the alternator **plus** ~37 A of solar.
- **Two mismatched arrays.** The roof panels and the detachable tent panels have different orientation and shading. One combined charger has a single solar input, and Redarc requires matched panels. Separate MPPTs track each array on its own.
- **Home Assistant.** The Victron units all report through the Cerbo GX (and the HA *Victron BLE* integration lists the Orion XS). Redarc/Projecta have Bluetooth apps only, with no HA integration found.

A combined unit only makes sense with a single matched array where saving space matters most, which isn't this build.

## 3. Rear control panel: 4 × USB-C

| Row | Contents |
|---|---|
| Top | GX Touch 50 · battery isolate switch · inverter on/off switch |
| Middle | IP66 double GPO · **2 × dual USB-C PD sockets (4 ports)** |
| Bottom | 2 × Anderson SB50 · 12 V socket |

- **Socket choice:** dual USB-C sockets in a ~29 mm round cut-out, e.g. Campervanbuilders dual USB-C 120 W (100 W + 20 W). The power-slide pod also gets a dual USB-C.
- **Watch the 12 V rating.** Many sockets only reach their headline PD wattage at 24 V; Offgridtec, for example, does 60 W at 24 V but 36 W at 12 V. Check the 12 V figure before buying.
- No 4-port USB-C PD panel socket exists. The Outcamp 4-port is 2 × C + 2 × A at ~38 W total.

## 4. Upper drawers: removable dividers

- Both upper drawers have **aluminium U-channel slots every 120 mm** on both inner faces.
- **9 mm ply dividers lift in and out** with the drawer pulled out. No tools; the lid stays shut.
- Three dividers are modelled per drawer; add or move them as needed.
