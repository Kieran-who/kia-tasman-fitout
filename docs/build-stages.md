# Build stages

The fit-out is split into seven stages, so it can be paid for over time. Each stage leaves the ute usable. Each one also puts in the things the later stages depend on (cable runs, mounting points, panel cut-outs), so nothing has to be pulled out and redone.

Each stage's parts and cables are listed in `bom/product-list-v0.13.csv` (column `stage`) and `bom/stage-map.csv`. The totals use today's prices, and many of those are sale prices (see "Timing purchases" below).

## Why this order

1. **Power first.** The front battery bay, the busbars and the rear control cabinet set where everything else goes, and every later circuit starts there. It also gives the biggest win straight away: a fridge and 12 V that run all trip.
2. **Roof next.** The platform has to go on before the visor, Starlink and roof solar. Fitting it means dropping the headlining. Doing it early, while the roof cables are pulled through once, saves a second headlining job later. Solar also keeps the stage 1 batteries full when parked.
3. **Slides and storage** are the biggest fabrication job but need no other stage. They come third so the electrics are proven before the tub fills up.
4. **All 230 V at once.** The electrician comes once, for the inverter, every outlet, shore power and the gap junction, and gives one compliance certificate.
5. **Tent solar, the cabin compute feed and under-tent storage** are add-ons. Stage 6's only electrical link is the water-can pad's 12 V from the tent services box, so it can be done in any month the budget allows.
6. **Visor last.** It needs the roof measurements and a prototype before any parts are ordered, and it's the most optional part of the build.

## Stage 0: measure (free, do first)

These measurements fix the `[EST]` dimensions in `cad/params.py` before any material is cut:
- tub floor height, arch well and tie-down positions;
- Load-Lid bar spacing and how far the tent bolts hang below them;
- Destroyer rail height;
- roof crown, platform height and the windscreen header (for the visor).

Also confirm the fridge model.

## Stage 1: power core

**Buy:**
- both iTech400X Pro batteries, with their MRBF fuses;
- busbars, the ML-RBS remote battery switch and its rear-panel switch, the SmartShunt;
- the Orion XS DC-DC charger, with the alternator cable, its 60 A MIDI fuse and the ignition add-a-fuse;
- the FB1 12-way fuse block;
- the rear-panel outlets: two Andersons, USB-C and a 12 V socket;
- the factory-outlet gland plate;
- the extinguisher and its bracket;
- the cold-weather kit for the bank: foam set, two 50 W heater pads with cut-outs, the TH1 thermostat / relay box, the F18 holder and the Victron temperature sensor (about A$242; [`cold-weather.md`](cold-weather.md)).

**Build:** the battery bay frame and cover, the rear control cabinet with its hinged panel, and the cable tray. Bond the pads and fit the foam before the batteries go in: neither can be reached once the bank is strapped down.

**You get:**
- the fridge on the rear Anderson;
- 12 V and USB-C at the rear panel;
- charging from the alternator;
- battery isolation from the rear panel;
- the SmartShunt and Orion monitored on your phone over Bluetooth;
- a bank that keeps itself above +3 °C on snow trips, with battery temperature on the SmartShunt. Set the Orion XS's low-temperature cut-off now (DVCC comes with the Cerbo in stage 4).

**Prepare for later:**
- **Leave space in the bay** for the MultiPlus and the Class-T fuse.
- **Fit the busbars in their final positions.**
- **FB1 comes complete:** ways 6–10 stay empty until their stages. Way 5 is a spare (the power pod is fed from its own MRBF fuse in the bay, stage 3).
- **Cut the rear panel now:** the GX Touch, GPO, inverter and visor switch holes, with blanking plates fitted.
- **Fit the conduits now:** the black NW48 split conduit and the orange 230 V conduit down the wall cavity and along the rail (runs R14–R17, sheet E12), on P-clips into rivnuts, with draw-cords to the gap junction and the cabin, so later cables just lay in.
- **Cut the gland plate with all three grommet rows** (one 230 V, two 12 V; sheet E11) and the cabinet's 12 V slot and 230 V gland.

**To shrink this stage:** buy one battery now and the second in any later stage. This drops stage 1 to about A$6.6k. Use the same model, and charge both to 100% on their own before connecting them in parallel. The cable lengths are already matched for two.

## Stage 2: roof platform and solar

**Buy:**
- the Wedgetail platform with its Tasman kit and solar brackets;
- the 190 W and 130 W panels;
- the **MPPT 100/30** for PV1 + PV2 and its PV isolator (the visor gets its own 100/20 in stage 7);
- the Starlink Mini mount.

**You get:** 320 W of roof solar and powered Starlink. The batteries stay charged while parked.

**Prepare for later:** while the headlining is down, also pull the **visor cables** (VIS 6 mm² twin, VSW 3-core and VIG) up to the roof. Coil them at the front cross-member. They cost about A$80 and save dropping the headlining again in stage 7. Add a spare 2-core for the light bar too.

## Stage 3: slides and storage

**Buy:**
- the DZ9308 46" runners (get a quote first: about A$2.1k of this stage is estimated);
- L-track, receivers and ball-lock pins;
- the leg hardware, the igus drag chain and its glide tray;
- the MRBF 30 A terminal fuse (F17) for the pod feed, on the system + busbar's spare stud;
- flush tie-down rings for the fridge-well pan;
- drawer runners.

**Build:** both slides with their decks and fold-out legs, the power pod (12 V side), and the over-arch and utility drawers. The drag chain lies flat under the power deck on its tray; its feed comes from the bay down behind the inverter into the front of the slide.

**You get:**
- both slides working, with the receiver grid and legs;
- 12 V and Anderson on the power pod;
- all the drawer storage.

**Prepare for later:** lay the pod GPO's chain-rated 230 V cable (AC3) through the drag chain now, with the 12 V feed. Leave it unterminated and labelled, and leave the GPO hole in the pod blanked. Re-threading a loaded drag chain later is the job to avoid. Check with your electrician first that they're happy to terminate cable you've laid.

**To spread it further:** build the power slide first (3a) and the driver slide later (3b). Their parts are the same, so each costs about half.

## Stage 4: 230 V and monitoring

**Buy:**
- the MultiPlus-II with its Class-T fuse and fan;
- the RCBO enclosure with the 2-pole main switch Q0 and the RCBOs;
- the outlets: rear double GPO, pod GPO and tent heater outlet;
- the shore inlet and its 2-pole RCBO;
- the gap junction box with all four connectors (J1–J4) and the tent harness;
- the Cerbo GX, GX Touch 50 and VE cables;
- the inverter switch.

**Labour (not in the parts total):** a licensed electrician installs and certifies all the 230 V work in one visit (AS/NZS 3000 / 3001.2).

**You get:**
- 230 V at the rear, on the slide and at the tent;
- shore charging through the tent inlet;
- the tent's 12 V supply through the gap junction;
- the full Victron system on the GX Touch, in Home Assistant and on VRM.

## Stage 5: tent solar and cabin compute feed

**Buy:** the two Sunman eArc 100 W panels on an ACM sheet, the MPPT 75/15, its PV isolator, the grey Anderson (J4), and the cabin feed: 6 mm² to an SB50 inlet on the under-seat compute box.

**You get:**
- **More solar:** 200 W from the tent top, on its own charger.
- **Compute feed:** a fused 15 A feed under the rear seat, ready for the smart-ute PC and network (docs/smart-ute-research.md). The cabin gets no outlets; the tablet runs from the Tasman's own USB-C.

## Stage 6: under-tent storage

**Buy:** rivnuts and rail clamps for the cross-bars, the drawer runners, the 30 L water can, the RotopaX diesel pack and straps, and the water-can cold kit: jacket, 50 W pad, TH2 thermostat, F19 holder and the XW plug (about A$170).

**Build:** the cross-bars, the drawer cassette and the drawer. The table you already own goes in the drawer.

**When:** the only link to the others is the water pad's 12 V from the tent services box (stage 4). Do the rest whenever there's a spare month.

## Stage 7: visor and roof extras

**Buy:**
- the visor parts: panel, rails, gearmotor, belts, tilt drives, latches, switches, controller and drag chain;
- the visor's own **MPPT 100/20**, its PV isolator QP3 and a VE.Direct-USB lead;
- the light bar and cargo bars.

**Before ordering:** the roof measurements from stage 0, and a bench prototype of the slide-and-tilt mechanism.

**When:** the light bar and cargo bars can move up to stage 2 if the budget allows.

## Timing purchases

- **Prices move a lot.** On 2026-10-20:
  - the batteries were A$2,099 each against a usual A$3,499;
  - most Victron gear was about 35% under RRP.

  Buying the big items on sale, even a stage early, can save more than spreading the cost. Store LiFePO₄ at about 50% charge, and keep receipts for the warranty dates.
- **Get quotes** for the large estimates before each stage:
  - DZ9308 runners: stage 3;
  - ball-lock pins in bulk: stage 3;
  - visor drives: stage 7.
- **Not in these totals:** labour (the electrician in stage 4), freight, tools, a fridge and Starlink hardware.
